How Often Should Men Get a Haircut? A Practical Grooming Schedule

September 9, 2026 · 5 minute read

There is no perfect haircut schedule for every man. The right answer depends on your hairstyle, hair growth, beard routine, and how polished you prefer the haircut to look between appointments.

Fades usually show regrowth sooner

Skin fades and higher-contrast fades make new growth easier to notice because the shortest sections begin so close to the skin. Many clients who like a consistently sharp fade choose more frequent maintenance.

For a deeper breakdown, read How Often Should You Refresh a Fade?.

Tapers and softer fades can grow out more gradually

A taper or lower fade often keeps more weight around the sides, so the change between appointments may feel less dramatic. The right schedule still depends on your hair growth and how clean you want the neckline and edges to remain.

Longer scissor styles may need a different rhythm

Longer styles can sometimes go longer between full cuts, but they still benefit from maintenance when the shape becomes heavy, styling becomes harder, or the sides and neckline lose their structure.

Your beard can affect the schedule too

If you wear a shaped beard, the beard may need attention on the same schedule as the haircut—or even sooner around the lines and transitions. Combining both into one mobile appointment can make maintenance simpler.

Use your appearance as the real trigger

Instead of following a strict universal number, pay attention to when your haircut stops sitting the way you want it to, when the fade loses definition, or when the beard starts losing its intended shape.
